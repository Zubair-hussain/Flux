Why 4K Isn't Always 4K

By Zubair Hussain · 2026-11-25

When you save a video, the quality menu looks simple: 720p, 1080p, 4K. In practice the numbers can mislead. This guide explains how Flux decides what to offer, using results from our own tests in November 2026.

The short version
  • Flux only offers qualities the video really has, and never stretches a small picture.
  • Tall phone videos are counted by their short side: 720×1280 is a 720p source.
  • 4K files are far bigger than 1080p, and on most screens the difference is hard to see.

Flux only offers what the source really has

Flux reads the list of versions that a platform actually provides and shows only those, from 720p up to 8K. It never creates a higher resolution by stretching a smaller picture. A 720p upload stays a 720p download, because upscaling adds file size but no detail.

Vertical videos count by their short side

Phone videos are tall, not wide. A TikTok clip we tested measured 720×1280. The shorter side is 720, so Flux calls it a 720p source. Judging by the long side would label it “1280p”, which exists on no quality menu and would promise detail the video does not have.

What different platforms offered in our tests

  • Dailymotion sample: versions up to 4K.
  • Pinterest sample: up to 1080p.
  • TikTok sample: 720p and 1080p.
  • Instagram, Streamable and Loom samples: 720p only.

These are single samples, not guarantees. Each uploader chooses how much quality to publish, and platforms change often.

Why 4K files are so much bigger

In the same Dailymotion test, the 1080p version was about 61 MB and the 4K version about 216 MB, roughly 3.5 times larger. On a phone or a laptop screen the difference is hard to see, so 1080p is usually the better choice. Pick 4K when you will watch on a large screen or edit and crop the footage.

Why some files are MKV

At higher quality, many platforms deliver the picture and the sound as separate files. Flux joins them with FFmpeg into one file. When the two parts use different codecs, MKV can hold them together without re-encoding, so nothing is lost. VLC, mpv and most modern players open MKV directly.

Download only what you may keep

A public video is not automatically free to reuse. Save videos you own, that you have permission to keep, or that are licensed for it, and follow each platform's terms.
